How to Approach the Text

Depending on your background, you may choose to read this book in different ways:

  1. For General Readers & History Enthusiasts: Start with the Praefatio. It provides a narrative history of cosmic music from Pythagoras, Plato, and Boethius through Renaissance magic and early modern science.
  2. For Philosophers & Music Theorists: Focus on Liber Primus. It lays out the core principles — how musical keys, scale modes, chord intervals, and audio effects are assigned to astronomical positions and planetary alignments.
  3. For Software Engineers & Sound Designers: Jump to Liber Secundus. Here you will find technical diagrams, architecture flows, mathematical formulas, and the actual Python and SuperCollider source code powering the music engine.

Glossary of Key Terms

To help you navigate the foundational esoteric, philosophical, and technical vocabulary used throughout the text, refer to this quick reference guide:

Aspect
The geometric angle formed between two celestial bodies relative to Earth (such as a 90° Square or 120° Trine).
Hermetic
Relating to the philosophical, alchemical, and cosmic tradition stemming from texts attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, grounded in the principle of correspondence (“as above, so below”).
Kairós
An ancient Greek concept denoting an opportune, qualitative, or alignment-driven moment in time, as opposed to Chronos (quantitative, sequential clock time).
Macrocosm & Microcosm
The Hermetic doctrine that the grand architecture of the universe (macrocosm) and the individual human or observer (microcosm) are structural reflections of one another.
Monad
The supreme, indivisible unity or ultimate source from which all numerical, cosmological, and musical proportions originate.
Orb (Orb of Influence)
The allowable margin or tolerance of degrees before or after an exact astrological aspect within which two planets are still considered in active geometric relationship.
Sonification
The process of translating non-auditory data — such as astronomical positions, planetary coordinates, or orbital vectors — into sound or musical parameters.

Quick Note to the Reader

The title of this book as well as the sections, figures, plates, and schematics are in Latin as a tribute to the works of Robert Fludd, but you can find the English variations of these titles at the bottom of sections, figures, plates, and schematics.
